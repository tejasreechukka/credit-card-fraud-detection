Project Proposal

Working Title

Credit Card Fraud Detection: A Comparison of Manual and AI-Assisted Data Science Workflows

Background

Fraud detection is a classification problem in which fraudulent transactions are typically much rarer than legitimate transactions. This class imbalance makes evaluation and decision-making challenging.

Research Question

How effectively can machine learning identify fraudulent transactions, and does AI assistance improve the efficiency, reliability, or quality of the analytical workflow?

Objectives

1. Audit and understand the dataset.
2. Conduct manual exploratory data analysis.
3. Establish a baseline model.
4. Train and evaluate additional classification models.
5. Investigate the impact of class imbalance and decision thresholds.
6. Compare independent analysis with AI-generated recommendations.
7. Document limitations, ethical concerns, and practical implications.

Initial Hypotheses

- Accuracy alone will not adequately describe fraud detection performance.
- Improving fraud recall may increase the number of false-positive alerts.
- AI recommendations may be useful but will require independent verification.
- The manual and AI-assisted workflows may differ in speed, reliability, and analytical quality.

Scope

This project focuses on offline analysis of a public, labelled dataset. It does not claim to deploy a production fraud detection system.

Planned Deliverables

Source code, notebooks, experiment logs, model evaluation results, AI comparison evidence, and a final research report.
